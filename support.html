Scanning your network

A scan finds few or no devices.

NetSentinel can only see the network your iPhone or iPad is connected to. Check these:

  • Local Network access is on for NetSentinel: iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network. When it's off, Radar shows “Local Network access is off” with an Open Settings button.
  • You're on the same Wi-Fi as the devices you expect to see, not on mobile data or another network. Some routers put guests or smart-home devices on a separate network.
  • Guest networks, and many hotel, café and office networks, stop devices on the same Wi-Fi from seeing each other (client isolation). There NetSentinel may only find the router and your own device. That's how the network is set up, and NetSentinel can't see past it.
  • Some devices sleep or stay quiet until they're used. Tap Scan to scan again (on iPhone you can also pull down on Radar).

On a large network, NetSentinel checks the 256 addresses around your device, and the scan bar says “Scans … only”.

Why don't I see hardware addresses, or the maker of some devices?

iOS doesn't let apps read other devices' hardware (MAC) addresses, so a device's page shows “Hidden by iOS” next to Hardware address. NetSentinel names the maker from what a device announces about itself on the network, so a device that announces little shows Manufacturer “Unknown”. This is an iOS limit, not a setting you can change.

Likely cameras

What does “likely camera” mean?

NetSentinel flags a device as a likely camera, and alerts you, when it has a video-streaming port open (such as RTSP) and its maker is one NetSentinel knows for cameras or camera modules. The Cameras filter on Radar, and the likely-camera count on iPhone, also include devices that show only one of those signs. It's an educated guess from what the device shows on the network, not proof: a flagged device could be a video doorbell or a recorder, and a real camera might not be flagged.

NetSentinel found no likely cameras. Does that mean there are none?

No. NetSentinel only sees devices on the Wi-Fi network you're connected to. A camera on another network (such as a recorder's own wired camera network) or on mobile data, or one that only records to a memory card, won't appear. If you're staying somewhere you don't control, also look around the room, especially at anything facing the bed or the bathroom, such as smoke detectors, clocks, chargers and vents. If you find a camera you weren't told about, contact your host or the booking platform.

Tracker Finder

Tracker Finder shows nothing, or says Bluetooth is off.

Tracker Finder needs Bluetooth turned on and Bluetooth access for NetSentinel: iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Bluetooth. On iPhone and iPad it works while NetSentinel is open. iOS pauses Bluetooth scanning when the app is in the background, so keep NetSentinel on screen while you look.

What can it find?

AirTag and other Find My accessories, Tile, Samsung SmartTag and Google Find My Device tags. In Tracker Finder, choose a potential tracker (they're listed first) to lock on, then walk slowly and pause at each spot: the meter shows when you're getting closer, and above −55 dBm you're within about a metre. Check bags, coat pockets, car seats and under furniture.

If a potential tracker stays in range, NetSentinel alerts you after 10 minutes; change the time in Settings › Alerts. If a tag is yours, open it from Radar's Bluetooth list (the Bluetooth section on iPad) and tap This is mine.

An AirTag I know is nearby isn't flagged.

An AirTag or other Find My accessory that's near its owner sends the same kind of signal as the owner's iPhone, AirPods and other Apple devices, so NetSentinel can't tell it apart from them. It lists it as “Find My device (near owner)” with the other devices nearby and doesn't flag it, which is normal in public places. When a tag has been separated from its owner, its signal changes, and NetSentinel lists it as “Find My tag (away from owner)” with the potential trackers.

Shield and Location

Why does NetSentinel ask for Location?

iOS only shares the name of the Wi-Fi network you're on, and its access point address, with apps that have Location access with Precise Location on. NetSentinel uses them to tell networks apart, check the Wi-Fi's security and spot look-alike networks. It never reads or stores where you are.

To allow it: iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › NetSentinel, choose While Using the App and turn on Precise Location.

Can I use NetSentinel without Location?

Yes. Network scans, Tracker Finder, VPN watch and Encrypted DNS work without it. Without the Wi-Fi name, Shield skips its Wi-Fi security, look-alike network and router checks, and alerts about new devices pause on networks NetSentinel can't tell apart. Radar then shows “New-device alerts are paused on this network”.

Encrypted DNS

How do I turn on Encrypted DNS?

  1. In Shield, choose a resolver under Encrypted DNS, or choose Custom and enter a DNS-over-HTTPS address that starts with https://.
  2. Tap Install Encrypted DNS.
  3. Open iOS Settings › General › VPN & Device Management › DNS and choose NetSentinel Shield.

The card shows DoH ACTIVE once iOS uses it. Your DNS lookups then go to the resolver you chose, under its privacy policy. Resolvers marked BLOCKS TRACKERS also block ad and tracker domains across your device; if a site or app stops working, try Quad9 or Cloudflare, which don't block ads.

How do I turn it off or remove it?

To stop using it for now, choose Automatic in iOS Settings › General › VPN & Device Management › DNS. To remove it, tap Remove on the Encrypted DNS card in Shield. To switch resolvers, choose another one and tap Update resolver.

Notifications

I'm not getting notifications.

Turn them on in iOS Settings › Notifications › NetSentinel. In NetSentinel, Settings › Alerts shows Allowed when they're on. NetSentinel notifies you about new devices on your network, trackers that stay near you, risky services, likely cameras, and Wi-Fi or VPN problems, and marks the most serious ones Time Sensitive.

Tracker alerts need NetSentinel open. While it's in the background, iOS decides when NetSentinel may check your network (Background App Refresh must be on in iOS Settings › General), so some alerts only appear when you open the app. Every alert is also listed under the bell at the top of Radar, or in Alerts in the sidebar on iPad.

Your data

How do I delete my data?

Open Settings › Privacy › Erase all scan history and alerts, then tap Erase. This removes scan history, known devices, trusted devices and alerts from that device. While Settings › iCloud › Sync trusted devices is on, the devices you trusted are copied back from iCloud the next time NetSentinel syncs. Turn it off first to keep them off this device. To clear only alerts, open Alerts, tap ⋯ and choose Clear history. Deleting the app removes its data from that device.

Erasing doesn't change the trusted devices kept in your private iCloud. To remove one from iCloud, open it and tap Remove from trusted devices while Sync trusted devices is on; the privacy policy explains how to remove the rest.

Privacy

NetSentinel collects no personal data. It has no accounts, analytics or ads, and your scan results stay on your device. Read the privacy policy.
