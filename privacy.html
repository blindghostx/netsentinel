NetSentinel Privacy Policy

Effective 23 October 2026 · Luke Turner

NetSentinel checks your network and the Bluetooth devices around you on your own device. It has no accounts, no servers of its own, no analytics, no ads and no in-app purchases. We don't collect, store, sell or share your personal information.

This policy covers NetSentinel Security (“NetSentinel”), an app by Luke Turner (“we”, “us”). It explains what the app accesses on your device and why, where that information is kept, and how to delete it.

What we collect

Nothing. NetSentinel has no servers of its own and no accounts or sign-in. It contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or other third-party SDKs, and it never sends your scan results or any other data to us or to anyone else on our behalf. It doesn't track you across apps or websites.

If you allow it in your device's settings (on iPhone and iPad: Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements), Apple may share crash reports and app usage statistics with developers. Apple handles this under its own privacy policy, and you can turn it off there at any time.

What the app uses on your device, and why

  • Your local network (Local Network permission). To find the devices on the Wi-Fi you're using, NetSentinel contacts the addresses on that network, checks a set of common service ports and listens for the names and services devices announce (Bonjour). This shows each device's network address, its name, maker and model when the device announces them, and its open services. If you run a deep audit on a device, NetSentinel also reads the greeting its services send, to identify their software. This traffic stays on your local network. iOS doesn't let apps read other devices' hardware (MAC) addresses.
  • Bluetooth. To spot trackers such as AirTag and other Find My accessories, Tile, Samsung SmartTag and Google Find My Device tags, NetSentinel listens for the Bluetooth Low Energy signals nearby devices broadcast and measures how strong they are. It never connects to them. The list of nearby Bluetooth devices is kept only in memory while the app runs; NetSentinel saves the tags you mark as yours and the alerts it raises.
  • Location, only for the Wi-Fi name. iOS lets NetSentinel read the name of the Wi-Fi network you're on, and its access point address, only while it has Location access with Precise Location on, or while its Encrypted DNS is set up in Shield. NetSentinel asks for Location only to read these, so it can tell networks apart, check the Wi-Fi's security and spot look-alike networks. It never reads your location or coordinates. The network name and access point address are stored on your device. The network name can appear in alert text on that device, and while iCloud sync is on it's saved with each device you mark as trusted in your private iCloud (see iCloud). NetSentinel works without Location. Unless Encrypted DNS lets it read the network name, only those checks, and alerts about new devices on networks it can't tell apart, pause.
  • Camera. NetSentinel for iPhone and iPad doesn't use the camera.
  • Notifications. To warn you about new devices on your network, trackers that stay near you, risky services, and Wi-Fi or VPN problems. NetSentinel creates these notifications on your device.
  • Files you choose. If you export a PDF security report, it's created on your device and goes only where you share or save it. On a Mac, if you import a manufacturer list (oui.csv), NetSentinel reads only that file.

When NetSentinel goes online

Apart from Apple services such as iCloud and the App Store, NetSentinel connects to the internet only to:

  • load Apple's captive.apple.com page, to check whether a Wi-Fi network has a sign-in (captive) portal. The request carries no cookies or personal information;
  • on a Mac, download the public manufacturer registry (about 4 MB) from the IEEE website, standards-oui.ieee.org, only when you ask it to. Nothing is uploaded;
  • send your device's DNS lookups to the encrypted DNS provider you choose, only if you set up Encrypted DNS in Shield. The providers NetSentinel lists are AdGuard DNS, Mullvad DNS, Quad9 and Cloudflare, or you can enter your own. Your device sends those lookups directly to that provider, under the provider's own privacy policy. NetSentinel sends it nothing else, we have no arrangement with any provider, and you can remove it at any time (see Support).

To show names for the addresses it finds, NetSentinel looks them up through your device's own DNS resolver (reverse DNS), which asks your local network or the DNS server your device already uses, such as your router or your encrypted DNS provider.

iCloud

When you're signed in to iCloud, NetSentinel keeps the devices you mark as trusted in your private iCloud database (Apple's CloudKit), which only your Apple Account can access, so your devices agree on them. For each trusted device it stores its network or hardware address, the name NetSentinel shows for it, the network it's on (its Wi-Fi name and address range, or its router's address), the name of the device that trusted it, and when. We can't see this data, and Apple's iCloud terms apply. Alerts raised on an iPhone or iPad stay on that device.

On iPhone and iPad you can turn this off in NetSentinel's Settings › iCloud › Sync trusted devices. Turning it off, or erasing data in NetSentinel's Settings, doesn't delete what's already in iCloud. To remove one device from iCloud, open it in NetSentinel and tap Remove from trusted devices while Sync trusted devices is on. To remove all of NetSentinel's data from iCloud, use the iCloud settings on your device (Settings › your name › iCloud), where you can manage the data apps keep in your iCloud storage.

What's stored on your device, and for how long

NetSentinel keeps its data in its own storage on your device: the latest scan, a history of your last 200 scans, the devices it has seen on each network, the devices you trust, up to 500 recent alerts, and what it remembers about each network it has checked (its name, access point addresses, security type and when it was seen), plus your settings and the Bluetooth tags you mark as yours. On iPhone and iPad this storage is protected by iOS data protection. Older scans and alerts are replaced as new ones arrive; everything else stays until you erase it in Settings or delete the app. Your settings and the tags you mark as yours stay until you delete the app, or until you tap Not mine, keep watching on a tag.

Deleting your data

  • In NetSentinel, open Settings › Privacy › Erase all scan history and alerts and confirm. This removes scan history, known devices, trusted devices and alerts from that device. While Settings › iCloud › Sync trusted devices is on, the devices you trusted are copied back from iCloud the next time NetSentinel syncs. Turn it off first to keep them off this device.
  • To clear only alerts, open Alerts, tap ⋯ and choose Clear history.
  • Deleting NetSentinel from your iPhone or iPad removes its scan history, alerts and settings from that device.
  • For copies in iCloud, see iCloud.

Sharing and selling

We don't sell, rent or share your information, and we don't use it for advertising, because we never receive it. No analytics, advertising or data company receives anything from NetSentinel. Information leaves your device only in the ways this policy describes: to your own private iCloud, to the services listed under When NetSentinel goes online, as described under If you use NetSentinel on a Mac, or when you choose to share a PDF report.

Purchases

NetSentinel has no in-app purchases or subscriptions, so it never handles payment details.

Your choices

You can turn off any permission at any time in your device's Settings: Local Network, Bluetooth and Location under Privacy & Security, and notifications under Notifications › NetSentinel. NetSentinel keeps working with what's left: without Bluetooth it still scans your network, and without Location it still finds devices and trackers.

If you use NetSentinel on a Mac

On a Mac, NetSentinel also notices when the camera or microphone turns on or off and keeps a history of those changes on that Mac, with the app names macOS reports; it never records audio or video. Where macOS allows it, it also lists the network connections of other apps on that Mac. If you pair it with your iPhone or iPad, the devices exchange scan results, alerts and activity summaries directly over your local network, encrypted between your own devices and without passing through any server. The Mac can also send its alerts, which can include the Wi-Fi name, to your iPhone and iPad through your private iCloud database, and NetSentinel removes those older than 30 days when it syncs. To pair, your iPhone or iPad uses its camera only to scan the Mac's pairing QR code, and records nothing.

Children

NetSentinel isn't directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and its effective date. The policy in the app (Settings › Privacy › Privacy Policy) changes with the next app update.
